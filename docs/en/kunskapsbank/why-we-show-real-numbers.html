Why I show real numbers, not a claim

Methodology

Many smart energy control products advertise a generic saving - "up to 30%", often without explaining how the number was calculated, which house it applies to, or over what period.

The numbers on this page are instead taken directly from a real house's own operational log, using a formula simple enough to recompute by hand: for each day, what the purchased electricity actually cost is compared to what the same amount would have cost with an ordinary household's usage profile (more in the morning and evening, less at night), both including VAT. The difference is that day's saving from shifting consumption. Solar that went straight into the house is valued at what it would have cost to buy: spot price plus VAT, grid fee, energy tax and any time-of-use tariff. Summed over every day since the system started, that gives the total. The app and the web use the same method (smartbox/savings.py).

Changed 2026-11-08: before, the comparison was against the day's unweighted average price and solar was valued at the spot price only. That underestimated both the price timing and the value of solar compared to a real electricity bill.

That also means the number can be small in some periods - for example early in the heating season, before heat control has been running long enough for its effect to show clearly in the statistics. I'd rather report that honestly (and label it "early season" when relevant) than show a promise that can't be verified.

The export script that computes the numbers, and the tables they come from, are in the source code - anyone can read exactly how the figures were produced.
